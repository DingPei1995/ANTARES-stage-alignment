Stage and Optics Alignment

ANTARES (SOLEIL) · reference procedure for the next calibration, and where its errors come from · draft 2026-11-20

Idea

The analyzer collects electrons from one fixed point, the analyzer focus. The procedure first puts a sample reference point exactly there with the stage (no X-rays needed), then brings each focusing optic's spot onto that same point and focuses it. After that the optic positions are known for the analyzer focus, and because the working distance of an optic does not change, the optic position in focus on any other sample tells where its surface is along the beam. That gives the sample height, and the SN that puts the sample back on the analyzer focus. The rotation centres are measured last, by tracking one point through SRn and SRz turns with the zone-plate focus.

1 · SNChamber marks / camera line: sample top surface on the analyzer focal plane. record SNf
2 · ST, SZLaser through the viewport behind the hemisphere: reference point on the entrance-slit centre.record ST, SZ
3 · Zone plateSpot onto the reference point (ZPX, ZPZ), then focus (ZPS). record ZPX, ZPS, ZPZ
4 · CapillaryPosition and attitude: spot onto the reference point, then focus. record CPS, CPX, ZPZ, 2 angles
5 · RotationsSRn and SRz series, zone-plate focus and SPEM maps on one point. fit SRn and SRz axes

Steps

  1. SN on the focal plane. SRn 120°, SRz 0°, PIX = PIY = 150 µm. With the chamber marks / camera reference line, move SN until the top surface of the sample is on the analyzer focal plane. Record SN; with the sample height H known this gives SNf (SN of the standard riser top in focus) = SN − H.
  2. ST, SZ on the analyzer axis. Shine the laser through the viewport behind the hemisphere so that it comes out through the entrance slit and the lens. Move a sharp reference point of the sample onto the slit centre with ST and SZ (SN unchanged). Record ST, SZ: with the point's plate position (x, y) this gives ST0 = ST + x and SZ0 = SZ + y, the analyzer focus in stage coordinates.
  3. Zone plate. At 95 eV, move the zone plate across the beam (ZPX, ZPZ; realign the OSA) until the SPEM map shows the reference point at the piezo centre, then focus with ZPS. Record ZPX, ZPS, ZPZ (and OSA positions): ZPS is now the zone-plate position for the analyzer focus.
  4. Capillary. Same with the capillary: position and attitude (two angles) until the spot is on the reference point, then focus with CPS. Record CPS, CPX, ZPZ (the vertical motor is shared by the zone plate and the capillary) and the two angles.
  5. Rotation centres. Track the same point with SPEM maps through an SRn series (e.g. 100–150°) and an SRz series (e.g. 0 to −25°), SN kept, refocusing with ZPS. ST / SZ give the axes along the plane; ZPS gives the positions along the beam — the only way to see the SRz axis across the beam (2026-11-15: it was 0.97 mm off, invisible to ST / SZ readings and to the capillary).

Using the working distance: height of a sample from the optic focus

With the optic positions of steps 3–4 and a fixed working distance, a sample found in focus at another optic position ZPS has its surface at

Δy = s · (ZPS − ZPSref) along the beam  (s = optic sign, scale ≈ 1)

from the analyzer focus. The beam meets the surface at 45°, so the surface is ΔH = Δy / √2 off the focal plane along SN, and ΔSN = −Δy / √2 (ST compensating, so the point stays on the beam) puts it back on the analyzer focus; the optic then returns to ZPSref.

Reading precision: zone plate ±0.02 mm → ΔH ±14 µm; capillary ±0.10 mm → ±70 µm; eye on the camera line about ±50 µm. So the zone-plate focus measures the height better than the camera line; the capillary does not.

Height error or SRz-axis error? How to tell after an SRz turn

Computed with the stage model for the 2026-11-15 SRz series on Cav (zone plate, SRn 120°, SN kept): change of the prediction for an error of +0.1 mm in H (the sample believed 0.1 mm higher than it is).

SRz−5°−10°−15°−20°−25°, SN corrected
ST (SN kept)−17 µm−35 µm−51 µm−68 µmacross the beam −61 µm
ZPS (SN kept)+13 µm+27 µm+41 µm+57 µm+128 µm (off the analyzer focus)
  • An H error shows in ST and in ZPS together, both growing with sin SRz (ratio about −1.2 : 1 with SN kept). With SN corrected it puts the point off the analyzer focus by about √2·δH at any angle.
  • An SRz-axis error across the beam shows in ZPS only (ST changes to second order). That was the 2026-11-15 case: ZPS +0.11 … +0.29 mm off with the capillary axes while ST agreed within 1 µm. Explaining the ZPS by H would take δH = −0.56 mm, which would put ST 0.1–0.38 mm off: excluded.
  • The open SRz −25° point (SN moved, ZPS 0.14 mm short): δH = +0.11 mm would explain the ZPS but predicts 66 µm across the beam (22 µm measured) and does not fit the SN-kept series (best δH there: +0.04 mm). H is at most a third of it.
  • So after an SRz turn: fit δH to ST and ZPS together. If one δH explains both, correct H (and keep it for the next rotations); if ZPS is off with ST right, suspect the SRz axis or SN_f, not H.

Where errors come in

For the next calibration: check list, by step. “Effect” is what the error does to user predictions.

steperror sourceeffect / how to reduce it
1 · SN on the focal plane Judging the surface on the camera line by eye: depth of field, parallax, camera resolution. Offset of SNf (2026-11-14: −0.3 mm instead of 0). All heights and the “analyzer focus” share it. Repeat, approach from both sides, average.
The mark / line itself not on the true analyzer focal plane (when was it set, for which lens mode?). Systematic: data taken off the analyzer focus (distorted angles and energies). Check against spectra: sharpest angular features vs SN.
Sample height H not known (substrate thickness, glue, silver paint, plate riser tolerance). SNf = SN − H is wrong by the error of H (2026-11-14: 0.500 instead of 0.525 mm). Use a reference sample of known thickness.
Surface not parallel to the focal plane: SRz zero offset, sample tilted on the plate, plate not flat in the holder.The SN read depends on where on the sample one looks. Judge at the reference point itself.
SN direction not exactly perpendicular to the focal plane; SN backlash. Small ST / SZ coupling; always approach SN from the same side.
Temperature: cooling the manipulator moves the sample by tens of µm. Calibrate at the measuring temperature, after it is stable.
2 · Laser on the slit centre The optical (laser) axis is not the electron-optical axis of the lens (lens mode, deflector zero, alignment of the viewport and laser). ST0 / SZ0 offset: the beam spot is put on the wrong point. Check once with photoelectrons (map the reference point with deflector 0 at the slit centre).
Laser tilted with respect to the analyzer axis by α. An SN error δ (step 1) moves the laser spot laterally by α·δ. Align the laser on the axis; do step 1 first.
Size of the laser spot on the sample, judging the slit centre (along the slit especially), slit width. ±tens of µm in ST / SZ. Use a narrow slit and a small, sharp reference feature.
Refraction / reflection at the viewport, diffuse spot on rough or transparent samples. Biased centre. Prefer a metallic, sharp feature (gold pad corner).
Plate position (x, y) of the reference point from the photo. Goes fully into ST0 / SZ0 (±0.05 mm, 2026-11-14). Mark it on a microscope photo registered on the plate photo.
Piezo not centred (PIX / PIY ≠ 150 µm) during the reading.Direct offset; centre it.
3 · Zone plate Focus judged on SPEM maps: depth of focus, map step, drift during the map. Error of ZPSref → all heights from the optic shift by it/√2. Focus series, take the sharpest (fit), same criterion every time.
Photon energy: the focal length is proportional to E (f ≈ 0.256·E mm, 24.3 mm at 95 eV). 0.1 eV → 26 µm in focus. Use the reference only at the same energy; check the monochromator calibration.
Moving ZPX / ZPZ changes the beam direction slightly (zone plate / OSA not on the incoming beam axis). Beam not along the model's +y by an angle τ: a sample Δy along the beam is missed laterally by τ·Δy (10 mrad × 2 mm = 20 µm).
ZPS scale and backlash (2026-11-15: focus / ZPS = 1.015).Error proportional to Δy; approach from one side; fit the scale.
Spot on the reference point read on the map (map resolution, recognising the feature). ±5–10 µm; use the same feature as in step 2.
Beam pointing drift (undulator gap, mono, thermal load, top-up).Spot moves after the calibration: re-check the spot position with one map before a series.
4 · Capillary Large depth of focus: CPS focus only to about ±0.10 mm. Heights from the CPS ±70 µm; not good enough for the SRz axis across the beam (2026-11-14 / 15).
Attitude (two angles) sets the beam direction; any later tweak changes it. Same τ·Δy error as above, and a new reference: redo step 4 after every capillary adjustment.
The capillary does not refocus by moving along exactly the beam axis.Spot moves sideways while focusing: check the spot position after focusing.
Achromatic, but the spot size and position depend on the alignment and on the energy band of the beam. Check at the energy used.
5 · Rotation centres Tracking the point on SPEM maps (feature recognition, map step).±4 µm per map (2026-11-15); use correlation of the integrated maps.
SRz axis across the beam: changes ST only to second order (cos SRz − 1).Seen only in the focus: needs zone-plate ZPS at large SRz (−15 to −25°).
Rotation axes not perpendicular / vertical (tilt), run-out (wobble) of the rotations. Errors that grow with the angle; not in the model. Watch the residuals at the largest angles.
SN straightness / direction when SRz is turned (open point 2026-11-15: 0.14 mm along the beam, 20–25 µm across at SRz −25 with SN moved 1.5 mm).Correct SN at large SRz less exact: refocus and use the piezo step.
Height H of the tracked point, SNf and the optic scale enter the fit together. Correlated constants; the fit reports the least-determined combination: add sets that break it.
Extrapolation outside the measured SRn / SRz range.Measure the range that will be used.
Piezo directions and scale (PIX, PIY), backlash of SRn / SRz.Checked 2026-11-14 / 15; approach angles from one side.
Always The assumption itself: optic positions and working distance unchanged after the calibration. Any optic realignment, energy change (zone plate) or capillary tweak invalidates the reference: redo steps 3–4 (RealSampleCalibration, mode optics).
Plate seating after a transfer (height in the holder).SZ offset of all samples: one Found here corrects it.

Reminder. Steps 1 and 2 define the analyzer focus for everything after: their errors do not show in any residual (the optics and the rotations are fitted around them). Check them independently — the analyzer itself (sharpest features vs SN, photoelectron map at the slit centre) is the only absolute reference.
